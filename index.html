Part 3
Is it better to live in a city or in the countryside?

I don't think the question has a general answer, and I'd be suspicious of anyone who gives one quickly.

What I can say is that the trade-off isn't the one people usually describe. The common framing is opportunity versus peace, and that's partly true. But the thing I noticed after living in six cities in five years is that cities don't actually give you more opportunities — they give you more options, which is not the same. Options cost attention. You spend a lot of energy deciding, and very little actually committing.

Smaller places have the opposite problem: fewer options means you commit to the ones you have, and that can be genuinely better for doing anything difficult. The catch is that they're much less forgiving if you turn out to be different from the people around you.

So I'd frame it as: cities are better if you're still deciding who you are; smaller places are better once you know. The mistake is optimising for the wrong stage.

答题思路「more options ≠ more opportunities」这个区分是整段的价值所在。最后一句「优化错了阶段」是很漂亮的收尾。

Part 2
Describe a place you like to go to relax. You should say: where it is; how often you go there; what you do there; and explain why it helps you relax.

The place I'd describe isn't scenic. It's a particular stretch of riverside in Suzhou — maybe four hundred metres long, with a bench roughly in the middle that I've come to think of as mine.

I started going there about two years ago, during a period when work was going badly and I couldn't sleep. I'd walk there at ten or eleven at night. There were always a few other people — someone fishing who never caught anything, a couple arguing quietly. Nobody talked to me, which was the point.

What I did there was mostly nothing. Walked the same four hundred metres, sat on the bench, went home. But something in the repetition helped. When everything else was changing — job, city, plans — that stretch of river was exactly the same on a Tuesday as it had been in March.

I think that's why it works. It's not that the place is peaceful, exactly; it's that it's indifferent. It makes my problems feel smaller in a way that a sympathetic friend sometimes can't. I don't go as often now, but I still think of it as a place I could go.

Part 3
Should old traditions be preserved?

I'd want to distinguish preserving a tradition from preserving the practice of it, because those often conflict.

There's a strong case for keeping the knowledge. If a craft or a form of music disappears, you lose something you can't reconstruct from a description — the technique lives in people, not in documents. In that sense preservation is genuinely urgent.

The weaker case is the one that gets made most often, which is that a tradition should continue simply because it's old. Age isn't an argument; plenty of old things were bad ideas. And there's a real cost to this framing, because it tends to freeze practices that were always changing, turning something living into a museum piece.

So I'd say: preserve the knowledge, be sceptical of the obligation. The traditions that survive are usually the ones people keep choosing, not the ones they're told to keep.

答题思路「preserve the knowledge vs preserve the obligation」这个拆分是全篇骨架。最后一句「活下来的是人们仍在选择的东西」很有力。
换成你的情况这类「应该保留 X 吗」的题，核心动作是「把 X 拆成两种东西」，然后分别判断。

Is competition good for children?

It depends entirely on what the competition is about and who's watching, which is why I don't trust general answers.

Competition against a measurable standard can be extremely useful. It gives a child a clear signal about where they stand, and losing in that setting teaches something that praise cannot — that effort and outcome are correlated but not identical, which is an uncomfortable and necessary lesson.

In the early twentieth century, Alfred Wegener noticed that the coastlines of South America and Africa appeared to fit together like pieces of a puzzle. He proposed the theory of continental drift, arguing that the continents had once formed a single landmass and had since drifted apart. His evidence included matching fossil record across oceans and similar rock formations on opposite shores. The theory was largely dismissed for decades, because Wegener could not explain what force could move something as massive as a continent.

The answer came from the ocean floor. Mapping after the Second World War revealed a chain of underwater mountains, and scientists realised that new crust was forming along them. As magma rises and cools, it records the direction of the Earth's magnetic field at that moment — producing symmetrical stripes on either side of the ridge. This became strong support for the idea of tectonic plate movement.

Where plates meet, the consequences are dramatic. At a subduction zone, one plate slides beneath another, generating earthquakes and volcanic activity. Sediment carried by rivers is compressed over millions of years into sedimentary rock, and stratigraphy allows geologists to reconstruct the sequence of events. Radiometric dating then provides absolute ages, while the fossil record supplies a parallel timeline of life. Mineral deposits, often concentrated along ancient plate boundaries, became the practical incentive for much of this research.

In the early twentieth century, Alfred Wegener noticed that the coastlines of South America and Africa appeared to fit together like pieces of a puzzle. He proposed the theory of continental drift, arguing that the continents had once formed a single landmass and had since drifted apart. His evidence included matching fossil record across oceans and similar rock formations on opposite shores. The theory was largely dismissed for decades, because Wegener could not explain what force could move something as massive as a continent.

The answer came from the ocean floor. Mapping after the Second World War revealed a chain of underwater mountains, and scientists realised that new crust was forming along them. As magma rises and cools, it records the direction of the Earth's magnetic field at that moment — producing symmetrical stripes on either side of the ridge. This became strong support for the idea of tectonic plate movement.

Where plates meet, the consequences are dramatic. At a subduction zone, one plate slides beneath another, generating earthquakes and volcanic activity. Sediment carried by rivers is compressed over millions of years into sedimentary rock, and stratigraphy allows geologists to reconstruct the sequence of events. Radiometric dating then provides absolute ages, while the fossil record supplies a parallel timeline of life. Mineral deposits, often concentrated along ancient plate boundaries, became the practical incentive for much of this research.

二十世纪初，阿尔弗雷德·魏格纳注意到南美洲和非洲的海岸线看起来像拼图一样能对合。他提出了大陆漂移（continental drift）理论，主张各大陆曾经连成一整块陆地，后来才彼此分开。他的证据包括跨越大洋相互吻合的化石记录（fossil record），以及对岸相似的岩层构造。这个理论在此后几十年里基本被否定，因为魏格纳无法解释，什么样的力能推动像大陆这么巨大的东西。

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants convert sunlight into chemical energy, and it underpins almost every food chain on Earth. Yet the relationship between organisms is rarely simple. Symbiosis — in which two species live in close association — takes several forms. In mutualism, both partners benefit: certain ants protect acacia trees and receive shelter and food in return. Parasitism is the opposite: one organism gains at the other's expense, sometimes without killing it, since a dead host is a useless host.

Predators exert a subtler influence. By removing the weakest prey, they keep populations healthy and prevent any single species from dominating. When wolves were reintroduced to Yellowstone in 1995, the elk changed their grazing behaviour, which in turn allowed willow and aspen to recover along riverbanks — a chain of effects that ecologists call a trophic cascade.

Every organism is shaped by its habitat. A thick coat, a slow metabolism or an ability to store water may each confer an evolutionary advantage in a cold or arid environment. Migration allows animals to exploit resources that appear only seasonally, while hibernation permits them to survive the months when food is scarce.

Today the dominant pressure is habitat loss. As forests are fragmented, populations become isolated, and a species with a small range faces extinction from a single drought or disease. Conservationists argue that protecting connected corridors matters as much as protecting individual reserves.

    4.4 General Training Task 1：书信题型与风格

    【已核实】官方原文：给一个情境，写 >=150 词的信，题目给出三个 bullet points 要你涵盖。信的风格可以是 personal（私人）/ semi-formal（半正式）/ formal（正式），取决于收信人。

    官方举例（原文）：

    • 写给大学住宿管理员反映住宿问题
    • 写给新雇主反映时间管理问题
    • 写给本地报纸谈机场开发计划
    • 写给租房中介解决暖气故障

    官方要求的三种功能能力【已核实】：

  • ask for and/or provide general factual information
  • express needs, wants, likes and dislikes
  • express opinions (views, complaints, etc.)

    • 行业通行的「书信类型」分类（【部分核实】非官方术语）：

    类型示例
    投诉信 complaint投诉服务/商品/住宿
    请求/询问信 request / enquiry询问课程、住宿信息
    申请信 application申请工作、志愿者
    感谢信 thanks感谢接待/帮助
    道歉信 apology为失约、损坏物品道歉
    建议/推荐信 suggestion / recommendation向朋友推荐地点、向公司提建议
    邀请信 invitation邀请朋友参加活动
    说明/告知信 explanation / information向房东说明情况

    官方还强调【已核实】：信开头不需要写地址；不写 bullet points；语气（tone）要与收信人关系一致（Band 7 要求 "The tone is consistent and appropriate"）。

    4.5 写作四项评分标准与权重【已核实】

    官方名称（英文原文，务必用英文原名）：

    #官方名称Task 1 用Task 2 用
    1Task Achievement是（Task 1）-
    1'Task Response-是（Task 2）
    2Coherence and Cohesion是是
    3Lexical Resource是是
    4Grammatical Range and Accuracy是是

    权重规则【已核实】：

    • 每项标准等权（each of the criteria carry equal weighting），四项平均 = 该 Task 的分数；
    • Task 2 在写作总分中的权重是 Task 1 的两倍（"Task 2 contributes twice as much as Task 1"）。

    #官方名称
    1Fluency and Coherence
    2Lexical Resource
    3Grammatical Range and Accuracy
    4Pronunciation
    • 四项等权（"Each of the criteria carry equal weighting and the overall average gives the IELTS score for Speaking"）【已核实】
    • 来源：https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-speaking ；https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail
    • 官方对四项的释义【已核实】：

      • - Fluency and coherence：以正常语速流利表达、不过多犹豫；句子与观点逻辑有序；恰当使用衔接手段（连接词、代词、连词）使听者不费力。

      - Lexical resource：词汇广度与使用准确性/得体性；包括不会某个词时的替换能力（paraphrase）。

      - Grammatical range and accuracy：语法结构多样性 + 准确性/得体性。

      - Pronunciation：让听者不费力就能听懂的能力。

    • 【部分核实】口语不考「内容是否正确/观点是否深刻」，也不存在「词汇量分数」。Part 3 的抽象讨论考的是能否展开论证（属 Fluency and Coherence 的 "topic development"）。
    • 【已核实】评分方式：「考官在整个考试过程中评估」，最终按全程平均表现评分（band descriptor 附注 ii："A candidate will be rated on their average performance across all parts of the test"）。

    How to use

    每天怎么用这个站

    网站每天自动更新一期。下面是一套 20–30 分钟的用法，比「刷时长」有效。

    一、词组：先看文段，再看词表

    顺序很重要。先读下面的英文文段（不查字典，遇到陌生词先猜），读完再回头看词表——这时你会发现那些词在句子里有了位置。最后遮住中文，试着复述文段大意。

    为什么这样设计：孤立背单词的遗忘率极高，因为大脑记的是「词—释义」这一条极弱的连接。放回文段，它连上了语法、搭配和语境，连接变粗，才留得住。

    二、阅读：先读英文，翻译是你的「对答案」

    中文翻译默认是收起来的。请强制自己读完英文再展开——展开的作用不是学习，是校验你有没有理解错。如果某句你看懂了但翻译读起来不一样，那正是你该停下来看的地方。

    三、口语：一定先自己说，再看参考

    每期 3 道题：2 道小题（Part 1 风格，答 3–5 句）+ 1 道大题（Part 2 独白或 Part 3 抽象讨论）。先录一遍自己的声音，再对照参考答案。参考答案下面的「换成你的情况」是整块替换模块，把里面的例子换成你自己的真实经历——考官能听出背稿，但听不出「背了自己」。

    一个提醒：参考答案里我刻意留了一些口语化的转折（Honestly / I have to admit / That said），这些不是废话，它们正是 Fluency and Coherence 拿分的证据。

    四、词库底座怎么用

    词库按考试和主题分组，每组都配一段覆盖全部词条的文段。不要从头背到尾——先选你今天感兴趣的主题，读完文段再看词。跨考试的部分（比如 undermine 在雅思和 GRE 都出现）说明它值得优先掌握。
